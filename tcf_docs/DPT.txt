What is DPT?
The Doctor of Physiotherapy (DPT) is a 5-year professional degree
program that prepares students to become licensed physiotherapists.
It focuses on rehabilitation techniques, physical therapy methods, and
patient care.
DPT graduates are trained to evaluate, diagnose, and treat various
physical impairments and disabilities using non-invasive techniques.

Musculoskeletal
Physiotherapists who
specialise in musculoskeletal
injuries, work to improve the
mobility of joints, strengthen
muscles and reduce pain.
They promote recovery
through hands-on therapy, as
well as educating patients
about improving mobility.

Cardiology
Specialist cardiology
physiotherapists may work in
cardiac rehabilitation. They
support recovery after heart
surgery, heart attacks and
other cardiac related illnesses.
They will also provide patient
education around healthy
living.

Respiratory
Respiratory physiotherapists
help people with lung
conditions such as COPD and
cystic fibrosis. They use
hands-on techniques and
adjuncts to clear chests. This
support improved breathing
and gaseous exchange.

Neurological
Neurological physiotherapists
help people who have multiple
sclerosis, Parkinson’s disease
and strokes. They use their
knowledge of the neurological
system to provide treatment
to enhance lives, support
return to previous activity and
reduce complications.

Paediatrics
Paediatric physiotherapists
work with children to help
them develop and learn. They
support patients who have
multiple issues such as
cerebral palsy, developmental
delay and cystic fibrosis. They
use their understanding of
child development to provide
effective treatments.

Vascular
Vascular physiotherapists
work with patients who have
vascular diseases, including
peripheral artery disease.
They provide rehabilitation to
patients with linked
complications (e.g.
amputation).
